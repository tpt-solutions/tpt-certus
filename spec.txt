## 1. Executive Summary

`tpt-certus` is a pure-Rust, domain-agnostic, formally verified 3D spatial library suite. Unlike traditional rendering engines that prioritize visual fidelity or game-like features, `tpt-certus` prioritizes **mathematical certainty, deterministic behavior, and auditability**.

By leveraging the `tpt-telos` agentic compiler and its self-contained Fourier-Motzkin SMT-style solver, `tpt-certus` proves spatial relationships, collision detection, and state transitions correct against a two-layer specification: an **ideal real-arithmetic model** (QF_LRA) and a **sound floating-point realization** of that model with explicit, machine-checked error bounds. This is a stronger claim than "proven over the reals" alone — it means every guarantee is backed by a proof that also accounts for the arithmetic the CPU actually performs, closing the gap that undermines most formal-methods marketing in this space.

The result: to our knowledge, `tpt-certus` is among a very small set of 3D spatial libraries where every geometric guarantee comes with a machine-checked certificate stating exactly what was proven, over which number system, and within what bound — suitable as genuine evidence in DO-178C, FDA, and financial-model-risk review processes, not just an internal QA artifact. The single load-bearing distinctiveness is "over which number system, and within what bound": theorem-prover-verified 3D algorithms in exact arithmetic exist (e.g. Lean 4 verified CSG mesh intersection, 2026), and production Rust spatial indexes are common, but no surveyed library publishes a per-build, two-layer (ideal real + IEEE-754 realization with derived `ε`) certificate over the *executing* artifact. This positioning was re-confirmed by the competitive survey (Phase 1.7, spec Section 7; findings recorded in `todo.md`).

## 3. Architectural Principles

1. **Verification First, Rendering Second:** The mathematical truth of the spatial state is the primary output. Rendering is a deterministic, headless visualization of that proven state.
2. **Two-Layer Soundness:** Every contract is proven twice — once over the ideal reals (QF_LRA), once as a bounded-error statement about the actual f64 execution. A guarantee that only holds in the first layer is not shipped as a guarantee; it is shipped as a documented assumption until the second layer closes it.
3. **Compositional Verification:** Proof obligations over large structures (BVH, octree) are decomposed into per-node-type and per-level invariants that compose into the whole-structure guarantee. This is what keeps the self-contained FM solver tractable at scale instead of requiring external SMT solvers — see Section 5.2, including an explicit note on what "per-node" means and when proof work happens.
4. **Domain Agnostic:** `tpt-certus` contains no aviation-specific, medical-specific, or financial-specific code. It provides pure, generic spatial primitives, proven generically; domain logic lives in the consuming applications.
5. **Leverage the Ecosystem:** Consume existing, battle-tested TPT crates (`tpt-math`, `tpt-engineering`, `tpt-formal`) and elevate them with `tpt-telos` contracts rather than reimplementing them.
6. **No `unsafe` Rust — scoped to `tpt-certus`'s own crates:** Every crate in Section 4 is written without `unsafe`, and memory safety within them is guaranteed by the Rust compiler — a distinct, separately-earned guarantee from the spatial/arithmetic correctness guaranteed by `tpt-telos`. This claim does **not** extend to transitive dependencies (`tpt-math`, `tpt-engineering`, `wgpu`, etc.), which are not audited as part of this guarantee and may contain `unsafe` internally. The two are never conflated in documentation or certificates; where a dependency's own safety posture matters to a customer, it should be assessed separately.

## 5. The Verification Strategy

### 5.1 Two-Layer Soundness (closing the real-vs-float gap)

Every verified function in `tpt-certus` carries **two linked contracts**:

1. **Ideal contract**, proven over QF_LRA / exact rationals — this is what Fourier-Motzkin elimination actually decides.
2. **Realization contract**, proven over the compiled f64 semantics, stated as: *for all inputs in the declared domain, the f64 result differs from the ideal-contract result by no more than `ε`*, where `ε` is derived from IEEE-754 rounding analysis (interval arithmetic over the operation sequence in the function body), not asserted by hand.

`tpt-certus` extends `tpt-telos` with the realization layer: the ε derivation from the ideal contract plus the function's operation graph is built by us on top of telos's round-trip pipeline (the upstream IR today is QF_LRA over integer/real atoms and provides interval bounding only for nonlinear-integer products — no IEEE-754 float semantics — so the realization layer and its ε are *our* construction, not an existing telos feature). Authors write one specification, and the pipeline produces both proofs. **This auto-derivation is itself trusted tooling, not something separately re-proven per function call** — its soundness rests on our interval-arithmetic implementation being correct, which is exactly the property Phase 2 of Section 7 puts through independent DO-330 tool-qualification analysis. Until that phase completes, treat the ε-derivation mechanism as a documented trust assumption, not an independently-verified guarantee in its own right; the per-function ideal and realization contracts it produces are still individually checked by the FM solver.

> **Float typing reality check (v0.2.0, empirically re-verified):** `Float64`
> parses and the main `render_type` maps it to `f64`, but the *agentic*
> `telos build`/`transpile` path observed in practice renders `Float64`
> parameters as `i64` and drops struct fields referenced only by `invariant`
> clauses, so generated Rust for any float-carrying contract currently does not
> compile (Phase 1.2).  `tpt-certus`'s realization layer therefore cannot rely
> on `telos build` output as the executing f64 artifact until upstream codegen
> is contract- and type-faithful; it is specified here as our own layer that
> consumes verified `.telos` (ideal) contracts and our own f64 implementation
> with derived ε.

Where a function's structure makes an epsilon-fuzzy zone unavoidable (e.g., near-tangent ray-plane intersection), the fuzzy zone itself becomes an explicit, proven region in the contract rather than an undocumented gap — see Section 6, Postcondition 3.

This is the mechanism that lets Section 2's guarantees be stated in terms an auditor can check numerically (e.g., "containment error < 1e-6 units") instead of the unqualified "100% exact."

### 5.2 Compositional Verification (keeping the solver tractable at scale)

Fourier-Motzkin elimination is doubly-exponential in the worst case as variable/constraint count grows, which would make naive whole-structure proofs (e.g., "this entire octree has zero false-negative collisions") intractable at realistic scale. `tpt-certus` avoids this by construction — and, critically, **entirely at build time, with no runtime proof cost**:

- Verification targets the **construction and mutation functions** (`insert`, `rebalance`, `split_node`, etc.), not individual runtime tree instances. Each function carries a local invariant (e.g., "if the input node satisfies its AABB-containment invariant, the node(s) produced by `insert` also satisfy it") proven once, at build time, universally quantified over all possible inputs — the same way `ray_intersects_aabb` in Section 6 is proven once for all rays and boxes, not re-proven per call.
- A **composition lemma**, proven once, states that if every construction/mutation function preserves the local invariant, then any tree built by any sequence of calls to those functions satisfies the whole-structure property, by induction over tree depth.
- Whole-structure verification is therefore a small, fixed number of FM proofs over the construction/mutation functions themselves, plus one fixed composition proof — **not** one proof per node of a live tree, and not something that runs again when the AV simulation in Section 2 builds or mutates trees at runtime. Runtime cost is the ordinary cost of running already-verified code; no solver invocation happens at runtime.

Before this is load-bearing for the Phase 2 roadmap, we will benchmark FM elimination time (at build time) against the construction/mutation functions exercised by a representative BVH shape, targeting near-linear proof-time scaling through depth 12–16, the range needed for the AV-simulation and aerospace use cases. This benchmark is a Phase 1 exit criterion, not a Phase 2 assumption — see Section 8.

### 5.3 Audit Artifacts

The build process generates a **Proof Certificate** per build, produced by `tpt-certus-proof`, containing:
- The ideal contract and realization contract (with derived `ε`) for every verified function reachable from the build.
- The composition lemmas used, for any structure verified compositionally.
- A machine-readable manifest cross-referencing certificate entries to source locations and to the specific regulatory objective they support (e.g., DO-178C Table A-5, FDA design-control verification records).

If any proof obligation fails, the build fails: `tpt-certus-proof` treats an unproven contract as a hard CI gate, not a warning, and no Proof Certificate is emitted for that build. There is no partial-certificate or "best effort" mode — a certificate's existence is itself evidence that every obligation it lists was discharged.

This manifest format is designed in direct consultation with DO-330 tool-qualification requirements (see Section 7) rather than as a generic "proof dump" retrofitted to regulatory use.

## 6. Phase 1 Proof of Concept: Verified Ray-AABB Intersection

**Phase 1 Goal:** Implement and formally verify `ray_intersects_aabb` in `tpt-certus-spatial`, fully closing the real/float gap and the boundary-case gaps identified in review, as the template all later crates follow.

```rust
// tpt-certus-spatial/src/ray_aabb.rs

use tpt_math_geometry::{Vector3, AABB};

/// Represents the result of a ray-AABB intersection.
pub struct Intersection {
    pub t_near: f64, // Distance to entry point
    pub t_far: f64,  // Distance to exit point
}

/// Formally verified Ray-AABB intersection using the robust Slab Method.
///
/// # Scope note
/// All contracts below assume finite, non-NaN inputs (`ray_origin`, `ray_dir`,
/// `aabb`, `t_max`). Malformed (NaN-containing) inputs are out of scope for this
/// contract and are rejected upstream by `tpt-certus-geometry`'s construction
/// invariants, not re-checked here.
///
/// # tpt-telos Contracts — Ideal Layer (QF_LRA, exact rationals)
/// requires:
///   aabb.min.x <= aabb.max.x && aabb.min.y <= aabb.max.y && aabb.min.z <= aabb.max.z
/// requires:
///   t_max > 0.0 // Explicit domain bound: the ray is only claimed correct up to t_max
/// requires:
///   ray.dir.x != 0.0 || ray.dir.y != 0.0 || ray.dir.z != 0.0 // non-zero direction
/// ensures: // Valid intersection bounds
///   if result.is_some() {
///     let i = result.unwrap();
///     i.t_near >= 0.0 && i.t_near <= i.t_far && i.t_near <= t_max
///     // Note: i.t_far is intentionally left unclipped by t_max. An entry point
///     // within the view distance constitutes a valid hit, even if the exit
///     // point lies beyond the bounded domain.
///   }
/// ensures: // Origin strictly inside AABB => guaranteed hit at t_near == 0.0
///   (ray.origin.x >= aabb.min.x && ray.origin.x <= aabb.max.x &&
///    ray.origin.y >= aabb.min.y && ray.origin.y <= aabb.max.y &&
///    ray.origin.z >= aabb.min.z && ray.origin.z <= aabb.max.z)
///   ==> (result.is_some() && result.unwrap().t_near == 0.0)
/// ensures: // Origin exactly ON a boundary face is treated as inside
///   (ray.origin.x == aabb.min.x || ray.origin.x == aabb.max.x) &&
///   (ray.origin.y >= aabb.min.y && ray.origin.y <= aabb.max.y) &&
///   (ray.origin.z >= aabb.min.z && ray.origin.z <= aabb.max.z)
///   ==> (result.is_some() && result.unwrap().t_near == 0.0)
///
/// # tpt-telos Contracts — Realization Layer (f64, auto-derived ε)
/// ensures: // Bounded numerical error vs. the ideal-layer mathematical result
///   let ideal = symbolic_evaluate_ideal(ray_origin, ray_dir, aabb, t_max);
///   ideal.is_some() == result.is_some() &&
///   (ideal.is_some() ==>
///     |result.unwrap().t_near - ideal.unwrap().t_near| <= EPSILON_T
///     && |result.unwrap().t_far - ideal.unwrap().t_far| <= EPSILON_T)
///   // EPSILON_T derived by tpt-telos from the slab arithmetic's condition
///   // number; published per-build in the Proof Certificate, not hand-asserted.
/// ensures: // No false negatives within the bounded domain (t <= t_max)
///   let ideal = symbolic_evaluate_ideal(ray_origin, ray_dir, aabb, t_max); // re-bound: independent clause scope
///   ideal.is_some() ==> result.is_some()
///   // Achieved by removing the magnitude-based "near-tangent" shortcut entirely.
///   // f64 division of a nonzero-however-tiny direction component still produces
///   // a well-ordered (possibly huge, possibly Inf) t, which compares correctly
///   // against t_max and the other axes' bounds. Only an *exact* zero direction
///   // component takes the containment-check branch, so there is no artificial
///   // control-flow cutoff left to create a false-negative window.
pub fn ray_intersects_aabb(
    ray_origin: Vector3<f64>,
    ray_dir: Vector3<f64>,
    aabb: AABB<f64>,
    t_max: f64, // Explicit domain bound
) -> Option<Intersection> {
    let mut t_min = f64::NEG_INFINITY;
    let mut t_max_slab = f64::INFINITY; // Distinct name: avoids shadowing the t_max parameter

    // X-axis slab
    if ray_dir.x != 0.0 {
        let tx1 = (aabb.min.x - ray_origin.x) / ray_dir.x;
        let tx2 = (aabb.max.x - ray_origin.x) / ray_dir.x;
        t_min = t_min.max(tx1.min(tx2));
        t_max_slab = t_max_slab.min(tx1.max(tx2));
    } else if ray_origin.x < aabb.min.x || ray_origin.x > aabb.max.x {
        return None; // Exactly parallel to the X slab and strictly outside it
    }

    // Y-axis slab
    if ray_dir.y != 0.0 {
        let ty1 = (aabb.min.y - ray_origin.y) / ray_dir.y;
        let ty2 = (aabb.max.y - ray_origin.y) / ray_dir.y;
        t_min = t_min.max(ty1.min(ty2));
        t_max_slab = t_max_slab.min(ty1.max(ty2));
    } else if ray_origin.y < aabb.min.y || ray_origin.y > aabb.max.y {
        return None;
    }

    // Z-axis slab
    if ray_dir.z != 0.0 {
        let tz1 = (aabb.min.z - ray_origin.z) / ray_dir.z;
        let tz2 = (aabb.max.z - ray_origin.z) / ray_dir.z;
        t_min = t_min.max(tz1.min(tz2));
        t_max_slab = t_max_slab.min(tz1.max(tz2));
    } else if ray_origin.z < aabb.min.z || ray_origin.z > aabb.max.z {
        return None;
    }

    if t_max_slab >= t_min && t_max_slab >= 0.0 {
        let t_near = t_min.max(0.0);
        if t_near <= t_max {
            Some(Intersection {
                t_near,
                t_far: t_max_slab,
            })
        } else {
            None
        }
    } else {
        None
    }
}
```

## 7. Regulatory Roadmap (DO-178C / DO-330 / FDA / Financial Model Risk)

Formal proofs and Proof Certificates are strong *evidence* for certification but are not themselves a certification. To make the Section 2 claims fully true rather than aspirational, `tpt-certus` treats regulatory and competitive-positioning readiness as an explicit, staged engineering track:

| Phase | Deliverable | Objective Addressed |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Proof Certificate format finalized; `tpt-certus-proof` crate ships. Lightweight competitive survey of formally-verified spatial/rendering libraries conducted to substantiate or revise the Section 1 positioning claim. | Establishes traceable, machine-readable verification records (supports DO-178C Table A-5 objectives on requirements-based test/proof traceability). Confirms the "among a very small set" claim in Section 1 is accurate before wider external use. |
| 2 | `tpt-telos` itself undergoes tool-qualification analysis under DO-330 (TQL assessment for a verification tool used to eliminate/reduce verification activity), explicitly covering the ε-auto-derivation mechanism described in Section 5.1. | Makes `tpt-telos`'s output, including auto-derived error bounds, usable as qualified-tool evidence, not just supplementary documentation. |
| 3 | Independent audit of the FM-elimination soundness argument and the realization-layer `ε`-derivation, by a third party with DO-178C/FDA design-control experience. | Confirms the two-layer soundness claim (Section 5.1) holds up under external scrutiny before it appears in customer-facing certification packages. |
| 4 | Reference submission package (redacted) built against the Phase 1 ray-AABB primitive, as a template for customers' own submissions. | Converts the abstract claim "certifiable" into a concrete, reusable artifact customers can point their regulatory teams at. |
| 5 | Financial model-risk track: map Proof Certificate contents to a specific model-risk-management framework (e.g., SR 11-7-style effective-challenge and documentation expectations), in consultation with a financial model-risk practitioner. | Gives the Section 2 financial row an equivalent staged path to the ones aerospace and medical already have via Phases 1–4, rather than leaving it as an unstaged claim. |

We do not claim "regulatory submission ready" before Phase 3 completes, and we do not claim the financial use case has an established model-risk pathway before Phase 5 completes; until then, Section 2's rows describe verification evidence, staged toward but not yet equivalent to, a completed regulatory or model-risk submission.

## 8. Phase 1 Exit Criteria

Phase 1 is complete when all of the following hold, not just when the code compiles:

1. `ray_intersects_aabb` passes both ideal-layer and realization-layer proofs as specified in Section 6, including the boundary and near-tangent postconditions.
2. The FM-elimination scalability benchmark (Section 5.2) is run, at build time, against the construction/mutation functions exercised by a synthetic BVH at depths 4, 8, 12, and 16, with proof time and memory recorded — this is the evidence gate for committing to "self-contained solver, no external SMT dependency" as a permanent architectural principle rather than a Phase-1-only convenience.
3. A first-draft Proof Certificate is generated for the Phase 1 crate and reviewed against the DO-330 objective mapping in Section 7, Phase 1.
4. The two-layer soundness mechanism in `tpt-telos` (auto-derivation of realization-layer `ε` from ideal contracts) is demonstrated on at least two functions beyond `ray_intersects_aabb`, to confirm it generalizes rather than being hand-tuned to one example.
5. The Section 1 competitive-positioning claim is checked against the Phase 1 survey (Section 7) and revised if the survey surfaces a closer prior-art match than currently known.

## Appendix A: Revision History — Section 6 Contract (Phase 1 Ray-AABB)

Kept separate from the specification body (Section 6) so that section reads as pure spec rather than change narrative.

- **Boundary postcondition** (origin exactly on a face) — closes a gap the original epsilon threshold silently papered over: "inside" is now unambiguous at the edge.
- **Realization Layer with auto-derived `EPSILON_T`** — converts "the intersection is exact" into "the intersection is within a published, per-build numeric bound," a claim actually true of the compiled binary rather than only of the specification.
- **Explicit `t_max` domain bound** — the no-false-negative claim is only meaningful over a bounded ray length; without this, "tiny but nonzero slope eventually crosses the slab at an enormous `t`" made the claim unprovable over an unbounded domain.
- **Removal of the magnitude-based `TANGENT_THRESHOLD` shortcut in the control flow** — an intermediate draft narrowed the false-negative window by adding `t_max` but kept the magnitude shortcut, which left a thin but real counterexample band (origins within roughly `t_max · TANGENT_THRESHOLD` of the slab, on a tiny-but-nonzero-slope axis). Replacing the shortcut with an exact-zero check (`ray_dir.x != 0.0`) removes the cutoff entirely: IEEE-754 division of any nonzero divisor, however small, still produces a correctly-ordered `t`, so the no-false-negative postcondition now holds over the whole bounded domain rather than outside a residual band.
- **Independent `let ideal = ...` binding in each realization-layer `ensures` clause** — contract clauses are independent predicates; a binding made in one does not carry into the next, so each clause re-derives `ideal` rather than assuming shared scope.
- **Explicit NaN/finiteness scope note** — states outright that malformed inputs are out of contract scope (handled upstream), rather than leaving that boundary for a reader to infer.
